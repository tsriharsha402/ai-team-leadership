# Interview loop: AI engineer

Five stages, about four and a quarter hours of candidate time in total. Every stage has a
clear purpose, a trained interviewer, and a written scorecard submitted **before** the
debrief.

| # | Stage | Length | Tests (competencies) | Interviewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recruiter screen | 30 min | Motivation, logistics, communication | Recruiter |
| 2 | Technical screen: practical coding | 60 min | Production engineering | Senior engineer |
| 3 | AI system design | 60 min | LLM application engineering, production engineering, product judgment | Senior / staff engineer |
| 4 | Evaluation and debugging exercise | 60 min | Evaluation and measurement, responsible AI | Senior engineer |
| 5 | Collaboration and judgment | 45 min | Communication, product judgment, responsible AI | Hiring manager |

Questions and scoring anchors are in the [question bank](question-bank.md).

## Design choices

- **No trivia, no leetcode puzzles.** The coding screen is a small, realistic task
  (e.g. a rate limiter or an answer cache with tests), because that's the job.
- **An evaluation stage in every loop.** The biggest gap in AI engineering candidates is
  measuring quality, not building features. It gets a dedicated hour.
- **Tools allowed.** Candidates may use documentation and AI assistants in the coding and
  design stages, as they would at work. We assess judgment about the output, not memory.
- **No unpaid take-homes over two hours.** They filter out people with caregiving
  responsibilities or current jobs, not weak engineers.

## Fairness

- Every candidate for a role gets the same questions at each stage.
- Scorecards are written independently and submitted before the debrief; interviewers
  don't discuss the candidate beforehand.
- Scores use the anchors in the question bank, not gut feel; "culture fit" isn't a
  criterion (collaboration is, with examples).
- Interview panels include at least one interviewer from outside the immediate team.

## Decision

The debrief starts with each interviewer reading their scorecard, then discusses
evidence, not impressions. The hiring manager decides:

- **Hire:** evidence at the target level for every required competency, and no
  "strong no" on responsible AI or collaboration.
- **No hire:** otherwise. If the evidence was insufficient rather than negative,
  say so, and consider a follow-up interview on that competency only.

Candidates hear back within five business days of their final interview, either way.
