# Competency matrix

Six competencies, three levels. Used for hiring decisions, performance reviews and
promotion cases, so a candidate is assessed on the same scale they'll later grow on.

A level is reached when someone **consistently** shows the behavior, not once.

| Competency | Mid-level | Senior | Staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LLM application engineering** | Builds features with prompting, retrieval and tool use following team patterns; handles errors, retries and timeouts | Designs AI systems end to end; chooses between prompting, retrieval, tools and fine-tuning with clear reasoning | Sets architecture across teams; anticipates how model capability changes will reshape designs |
| **Evaluation and measurement** | Adds cases to eval sets; runs evals before merging; reads failures | Designs evaluation plans with thresholds and decision rules; knows when a result is noise | Defines evaluation strategy for the org; builds shared eval infrastructure; teaches statistical thinking |
| **Production engineering** | Ships reliable, tested, observable services | Owns latency, cost and reliability for a system; leads incident response | Designs for org-wide reliability and cost; sets standards others follow |
| **Product judgment** | Understands the user problem behind a task | Pushes back on AI where a simpler solution works; defines what "good" means with product | Shapes which AI bets the org makes, and which it stops |
| **Responsible AI and risk** | Follows the team's safety practices (abstention, citations, data handling) | Leads risk assessments; designs mitigations for hallucination, injection and privacy | Sets policy with legal, security and leadership; makes risk tradeoffs explicit at exec level |
| **Communication and collaboration** | Writes clear PRs and docs; asks for help early | Writes decision records and evaluation reports stakeholders act on; mentors | Aligns multiple teams and leaders; grows senior engineers |

## Using it in hiring

Each interview stage in the [interview loop](interview-loop.md) is mapped to two or three
competencies. The hiring decision needs evidence, at the target level, for every
competency the role requires; strength in one area doesn't cancel a gap in another.

## Using it in reviews

For each competency: current level, two or three concrete examples from the period, and
one growth goal. Examples are things that happened ("designed the eval plan for X and
caught Y before launch"), not traits ("is thorough").
