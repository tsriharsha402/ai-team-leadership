How I would hire, onboard and lead a team that ships AI features to production. This is
the people-and-operations companion to the
[AI Delivery Playbook](https://github.com/tsriharsha402/ai-delivery-playbook), which
covers *what* a team does to ship AI safely; this repo covers *who* does it and *how the
team runs*.

Everything here is concrete enough to use on Monday: rubrics with scoring anchors,
interview questions with what good and bad answers look like, and plans with dates.

## Principles

1. **Hire for judgment, train for tools.** Frameworks change every six months. Knowing
   when an AI answer is good enough, and proving it, doesn't.
2. **Evaluation is everyone's job.** No role on the team is exempt from writing evals or
   reading failures.
3. **Make quality visible.** Weekly eval reviews and published metrics beat heroics.
4. **Default to written.** Decisions, plans and postmortems are documents, so the team
   scales beyond the people who were in the room.
5. **Protect focus.** AI work invites endless experimentation; a roadmap says what we are
   *not* doing this quarter.
